------------------------------------------------------------
1. HOW THE CONTROLLER WORKS (plan to THIS interface)
------------------------------------------------------------

The simulator is KINEMATIC (no gravity). Each frame the coder's generator is
asked, at a scalar phase, for: the base pose, a target for each end-effector, any
directly-set joints, the current contacts, and any scene-object poses. The
framework then solves IK to the EE targets and — under "contact" grounding — drops
the base so the declared contacts rest exactly on their surface.

phase ∈ [0, 1]. A skill is EITHER:
  - CYCLIC  (a gait/dance that repeats seamlessly — phase 1 == phase 0), or
  - ONE-SHOT (a pick-and-place, a climb, a single jump — phase 0 is the start
    pose, phase 1 the distinct end pose; it plays once, it does not wrap).
State which in the "cyclic" field. Do not force a one-shot skill to be periodic.

Each end-effector, each phase, is driven by exactly ONE channel. Your job is to
decide which:
  - WORLD contact  — a support limb PLANTED at a fixed world point on a surface
      (a stance foot, a hand on the floor/ledge). It is listed in the contacts
      for that phase, so the framework pins it and grounds the base to it. A
      planted contact MUST NOT slide; a "pivot" contact stays at one point but may
      spin/roll about it (a pirouette foot).
  - WORLD reach    — a FREE limb whose position is FIXED BY AN EXTERNAL THING it
      must coincide with: a hand reaching onto a ledge top, or a hand that GRIPS /
      HOLDS / PUSHES an object (it sits AT the object's grip face). An absolute
      world target; IK solves the reach. Not load-bearing, so not grounded to — but
      a held object is placed AT this same point, so limb and object COINCIDE.
  - BODY swing     — a FREE limb moving RELATIVE to the base (a walking leg's
      swing, an arm gesture defined off the torso).
  - JOINT posture  — a FREE limb whose ANGLES are the point and whose endpoint is
      NOT fixed by anything external: a wide/deep stance IK would over-reach, an
      inverted or kicking leg, an expressive arm/torso/neck gesture. The coder sets
      joints directly and IK skips that limb.

  The discriminator is COUPLING, not the limb: a limb whose endpoint is fixed by
  the world or by a thing it touches/holds → world (or body) IK to that point; a
  limb whose shape is the point and nothing external fixes its endpoint → joint
  angles. Decide this per limb per phase from the skill; do not assume a fixed
  channel for any limb (a hand can be joint-driven for a wave and world-IK to grip
  a box in the same skill).

§1b. CONTACT REACHABILITY — the base and the contacts are ONE decision
------------------------------------------------------------------------
A contact is only real if the limb can actually GET there from the base you author
at that phase. The contact is fixed by the world; the base is your free choice, so
DERIVE THE BASE FROM THE CONTACT, never the reverse. A schedule whose contacts lie
outside the base's reach cannot be rescued by any amount of coder tuning — the limb
simply under-reaches and the contact floats, and the run is rejected before it is
even simulated.

{reach_envelope}

Apply this while you write the contact_schedule, not after. Two consequences worth
stating explicitly, because they are the usual failure:

  * A contact on a LOW surface may be unreachable from an upright base even though
    the limb is "long enough" — what matters is the LIMB ROOT's height, not the
    limb's length. If so, the plan must lower the base, PITCH the torso so the limb
    root comes down, or INVERT — and base_motion must say which.
  * Every phase must satisfy this for EVERY contact it declares AT THE SAME TIME.
    If two contacts want incompatible base heights in one phase, the schedule is
    wrong: stagger them into separate phases, or move the base between them.

§1c. FLIGHT IS BALLISTIC — gravity, not choice
------------------------------------------------------------------------
A phase with EMPTY contacts is unpowered. The base can then only follow

    z(t) = z0 + v0*t - g*t^2/2        (g = 9.81)

so the flight's DURATION and its height change are the SAME decision: pick one and
the other follows. A window longer than the fall it describes is the robot hanging
in the air, and support_or_ballistic rejects the entire window.

  * A pure DROP of h metres takes sqrt(2h/g) seconds — 0.75 m takes 0.39 s, not 1 s.
  * Pushing off first buys a little more: at this robot's maximum launch velocity a
    drop of h lasts (v_max + sqrt(v_max^2 + 2gh))/g seconds. Your jump height is in
    the physical summary; you cannot exceed it.
  * A flight window is usually SHORT. If the motion needs more time, put it in the
    powered phases before or after — not inside the flight.

Record the number you chose: each base_motion.per_phase entry MUST carry a numeric
"base_height_m", and each contact MUST carry a numeric "surface_height_m" (0.0 for
the floor; the exact tread/ledge/seat height from the scene geometry otherwise).
These are checked mechanically against the table above before any code is written.

The BASE may be given an ABSOLUTE world trajectory (height, forward/lateral
position, roll/pitch/yaw) — e.g. torso lowering into a squat, rising onto a ledge,
pitching for a dive. It need not be pure velocity integration. Say what the torso
DOES (height + orientation + where it travels), phase by phase.

This robot's default ground-support end-effectors are {support_set}; free
(non-contact) EEs are {free_ees}. A skill may bear weight on a DIFFERENT set
(hands for a crawl/handstand, rear feet only, one foot for a balance).

ANY body part may bear weight on a surface — not only the end-effectors. Besides
the EEs, these body parts may be DECLARED as contacts: {contact_bodies}. Use them
when the skill rests on them: a hands-and-knees crawl bears weight on the HANDS and
the KNEES (feet lifted), so its contacts are the hands + knees, NOT the feet. When
you declare a contact (any body), the framework seats it on the surface and the
tests REQUIRE it to actually rest there — so declare EVERY limb that bears weight,
and the motion cannot cheat by lifting a load-bearing limb off the ground. (A crawl
whose "hands" float at chest height is then impossible: if the hands are declared
contacts, they must be on the floor.) No body part, declared or not, may PENETRATE
a surface.

  HOLDS a height — a crawl/crouch.)

------------------------------------------------------------
3. FIDELITY INVARIANTS (you declare what "done right" MEANS)
------------------------------------------------------------

The unit tests enforce a PHYSICS FLOOR (nothing through a surface, contacts rest
or the base is ballistic, planted contacts don't slide, joints in range). Passing
the floor is necessary but NOT sufficient — a stiff shuffle that goes nowhere can
pass every physics law yet look nothing like the skill. So YOU declare a handful
of measurable FIDELITY INVARIANTS: geometric facts that must be true for THIS
motion to actually be the intended one. They are how the repair loop knows it is
getting closer to the skill, not just to legality.

Compose them from these GENERAL primitives (pick the few that matter for the
skill; give each concrete numbers from the physical summary):
  - net_progress   {axis: "horizontal"|"x"|"y"|"z", min, max}   base travels this far
                     NET (end minus start) — for a skill that ENDS displaced (a walk
                     moves forward; a climb ends higher: axis "z" net vertical RISE, set
                     from the scene's top-surface height minus the floor-start height).
                     Do NOT use net_progress for a motion that RETURNS (a jump, a squat)
                     — its end ≈ its start, so net is ~0 and it can never pass; use peak_rise.
  - peak_rise      {axis: "x"|"y"|"z", min, max, phase_window?: [a,b]}   the base's PEAK
                     excursion FROM ITS START — the right primitive for a motion that
                     rises/dips then RETURNS: a jump's APEX, a handstand's INVERSION
                     height, a squat's DIP. A POSITIVE band [min,max] measures the upward
                     rise (apex/inversion above start); a band with max<=0 measures a
                     downward dip (a squat, e.g. min -0.5 max -0.2). Use this, NOT
                     base_height_band, for any excursion.
  - swing_lift     {ee, min, max}          a limb clears the ground by this much mid-swing
  - phase_offset   {ee_a, ee_b, expected, tol}   two contacts' onset timing (gait
                     coordination — e.g. a diagonal pair is antiphase, expected 0.5)
  - base_height_band {min, max}            the torso HOLDS this height band for the WHOLE
                     skill — declare it ONLY for a sustained height (a crawl stays low, a
                     march stays tall). NEVER for a motion that both stands AND
                     dips/rises (a squat, a jump, an inversion): the band is checked over
                     the entire trajectory, so the excursion or the standing bookends
                     always violate it — use peak_rise for those.
  - stance_no_drift {ee, max_drift}        a specific planted contact barely moves
  - object_coincident {object, ee, max_dist}   a held object rides with its holder
  - contact_over_surface {ee, x_range, y_range, phase_window, tol}
                     a contact body gets HORIZONTALLY OVER a surface's footprint.
                     REQUIRED whenever the goal puts a body part ON a surface. A HEIGHT
                     band cannot express this: matching a surface's height is satisfied
                     just as well BESIDE it as ON it, so a motion that stops short and
                     holds the right height scores clean without ever arriving. Height
                     says how low; this says WHERE. Take x_range/y_range from the scene
                     geometry's own footprint for the surface you chose.
  - end_state      {mode: "advances", min_advance} | {mode: "returns"}   one-shot
                     ends distinct / travels, or a cyclic skill returns to its start

Rules:
- Declare every invariant that DISTINGUISHES this skill from a degenerate motion
  (a walk that must MOVE; a crawl's DIAGONAL timing; a carry's object staying WITH
  the hands; a squat's LOW dip) — and no more. How many that is depends on the
  skill: one can suffice, some carries need several. Do not pad to a quota; a noise
  invariant misdirects the repair loop.
- Numbers come from the physical summary and your plan (reach, stride, speed).
- DERIVE every band from GEOMETRY — never pick a "safe" round number. A band that
  contradicts the scene or the robot's reach is WORSE than no band: the coder may not
  edit it, so it will SACRIFICE real contact to satisfy your number (walk short of a
  chair to stay inside a travel band, shrink a crouch to stay inside a base band). Rules:
  * A band that ends AT an object must be computed FROM that object's own coordinates
    as given in the scene geometry above — never a round number picked for safety. If a
    support surface spans x∈[a,b], the final base/body x-band is an interval INSIDE
    [a,b], not one that stops short of a. If an object to be gripped rests at x=c and
    the limb reach is R, the stance x-band must put the limb root within R of c, i.e.
    CLOSE ENOUGH to grip — never a stance a full reach away from the object.
  * Every DECLARED CONTACT must be REACHABLE from the base you plan at that phase (base
    within one limb-length of the contact) AND, when it rests on a named surface, its
    (x,y) must lie WITHIN that surface's footprint. A pelvis "on the seat" authored over
    the bare floor 30 cm short is a FLOATING contact — it fails contact_grounded no matter
    what the fidelity bands say. Check reach + footprint for every contact as you schedule it.
  * Bands must be MUTUALLY satisfiable: a "pick the floor box" crouch band and a "barely
    move forward" travel band that cannot both hold at a reachable stance is a contradiction
    — widen the travel so the stance actually reaches, don't shrink the crouch.
  * SWING CLEARANCE OVER AN OBSTACLE: when a limb must swing UP onto a raised surface, its
    swing_lift MIN derives from the RISE it must clear = (target surface top − the surface it
    started on) + a small margin, MEASURED FROM THE START SURFACE (a foot going floor->0.75 m
    top lifts ~0.8 m, i.e. peak z ~0.85). NEVER cap a swing_lift below that (a [0.15,0.45] band
    is impossible and the foot clips the face); on raised terrain declare swing_lift MIN-only.
    But NEVER ADD clearance on top of the LANDING height (peak = landing + margin is wrong): a
    foot CANNOT rise ABOVE its own HIP — cap any swing peak at the planned base/pelvis z minus a
    margin. A foot target above the hip is unreachable; IK saturates and the leg falls INTO the
    obstacle (the climb foot 0.4 m above the hip ended up 35 cm inside the slab).
  * A phase_offset "expected" is UNSIGNED cyclic distance in [0, 0.5] (0 = in phase, 0.5 =
    antiphase) — never a signed lead/lag. A diagonal-trot pair is ~0.5; declare 0.5, not −0.5.
- These are YOURS: only you (the planner) set them. The coder may never edit them.
  If a later re-plan shows an invariant was itself wrong (a core check fails in a way the
  band forbids fixing), YOU re-declare it — a band that fights a core physics check is
  always the band's fault, re-derive it from the geometry.

If any contact LEGITIMATELY slides (a moonwalk, a drag step, a curling stone),
list its end-effector name in "sliding_contacts" — that, and only that, exempts it
from the no-slide law. Default is empty: a planted contact must not slide.

4. COMMON-SENSE PHYSICS THE PLAN MUST RESPECT
------------------------------------------------------------

- NON-PENETRATION (a hard law, always enforced): NO part of the robot — not an
  end-effector, and not a knee, shin, elbow, or the torso — may pass BELOW the
  floor or INTO any object surface (a step, a slab, a wall). Every contact rests
  ON a surface, never through it. Plan the base height and every contact from the
  EXACT surface heights given in the scene geometry, and route swing limbs so their
  path stays ABOVE/AROUND obstacles (rise before you advance over a step; do not
  send a foot diagonally through a slab's front face). A motion that only "reaches
  the top" by clipping through the obstacle is a FAILURE.
- A planted contact does not slide (a wheel may roll; a pivot may spin in place).
- The robot stays SUPPORTED (some contact resting on a surface) except during a
  genuine ballistic jump/flip, where the base rises then falls and RE-LANDS. When
  the support set CHANGES (a step, or feet->hands in a handstand), OVERLAP the
  handoff: the new contact arrives before the old one leaves, so some contact
  rests at every instant. A far target (a hand on the floor while inverted) must be
  within the limb's reach or it floats — plan the base height so the limb root
  comes to arm's length of the contact.
- CONTACT-RELEASE LAW (mutual reachability): a planted contact BOUNDS the base — the
  base can only go where every currently-planted limb can still reach its contact. So
  before the base TRANSLATES or RISES beyond a planted contact's reach ball, you must
  RELEASE that contact first (lift and re-plant it onto the new surface). NEVER keep a
  contact planted "for stability" once the base has moved past its reach: the limb
  cannot stretch there, so the IK either under-reaches (the contact floats) or the
  framework drags the base back onto the contact (a penetration cascade — the climb
  that held a floor foot while rising onto a ledge sank its shin THROUGH the ledge).
  On a step-up/climb: plant hands/upper limbs on the new surface, THEN release the
  trailing feet from the floor and swing them up — a foot leaves the low surface the
  instant the base rises above its reach, it is not glued there.
- GOAL-STATE TOPOLOGY (general — pick the one the SKILL INTENT names; the ENVIRONMENT only
  supplies the surfaces + numbers, never the goal). A scene object is a SOLID: its TOP is a
  support surface, its FACES are collision walls, it is solid to the floor. What the robot DOES
  with it is one of these general end-states — choose from the skill's words, take all
  coordinates from the scene geometry, and make your TERMINAL contact_schedule satisfy it:
  * REST / PERCH / SIT / LIE on a surface: ONE designated body part (the pelvis for a sit, the
    torso for a lie) rests ON the surface (z = surface_top + its offset), INSIDE the footprint;
    the OTHER weight-bearing contacts (the feet) STAY on their LOWER support surface (the floor,
    or a footrest/rung if the seat is too high to reach the floor) and IN FRONT of the object's near FACE. Weight TRANSFERS to the new surface: the base moves
    so the resting part is over the surface while the other contacts stay put — do NOT keep them
    under the base (that is a SQUAT ON the object, not a rest — a FAILURE). A foot authored "on
    the floor" INSIDE a solid object's footprint is driven onto its top: keep feet OUTSIDE the
    footprint, on the approach side of the face.
  * MOUNT / CLIMB / STEP / GET ON TOP of a surface: ALL weight-bearing contacts END on the
    target surface (z = top + offset). While ascending, any contact still below the top stays on
    the APPROACH side of the face (never crosses it below the top); release a trailing lower
    contact BEFORE the base rises past its reach.
  * CARRY / PICK / MOVE an object: grip it with designated limbs (world-frame) AT its actual
    faces, declare it held (attachments), move together, release where the goal says. The grip
    must be REACHABLE from the base you plan — position the base in BOTH height and distance so
    each gripping limb's ROOT (the shoulder) comes within arm's reach of the object's grip point,
    WHEREVER the object is. Read the object's height from the scene geometry and place the base to
    it: a LOW object (on the floor) -> lower/pitch the base DOWN to it; a WAIST/TABLE-height object
    -> reach FORWARD at that height (little or no crouch); a HIGH object (a shelf/ledge top) ->
    RAISE/extend the base UP to it. The invariant is always "limb-root within arm's reach of the
    grip," never a fixed crouch or a fixed stance. A grip authored beyond arm's reach does NOT hold
    — the hand stops short and the object TELEPORTS to it. Getting within reach of the grip (at the
    object's real height) OUTRANKS a tidy travel band.
  In EVERY case: each contact's declared `surface` names where it rests; author its z from THAT
  surface; a contact placed on the wrong-height surface (a rest's feet on the seat, a mount's
  foot left on the floor) FAILS. The topology is the SKILL's, not the object's — the same object
  can be sat on OR climbed on; read the intent.
- A STEPPING gait lifts and re-plants its swing contacts (they clear the ground
  and set back down); a planted contact is never glued or dragged UNLESS declared
  sliding. (This is about lift/re-plant, not a fixed step direction or posture — a
  backward walk lands behind the hip, a drag step slides by declaration.)
- Every contact make/break and control-channel change is a PHYSICAL EVENT with
  WIDTH — schedule it over a nonzero phase window (an overlap), never as an
  instantaneous flip. A limb that switches between joint-driven and IK-driven at a
  hard boundary teleports the joints (fails `no_teleport`); give the switch room and
  make the swing arrive at its foothold before it is declared a contact.
- DURATION IS PHYSICS — set "duration_s" so no joint has to move faster than its
  ceiling. Phase is normalized time; the ACTUAL joint speed is (radians a joint
  travels in a window) / (that window's SECONDS), and a window's seconds =
  duration_s * its phase-width. So a big motion (a leg swinging a full stride, a
  torso pitching down to a box) needs enough duration_s or it exceeds the velocity
  envelope and teleports. Rule of thumb: a stance step of a walk/climb takes ~1 s,
  so an N-step ascent needs duration_s ≈ N seconds; a pick-carry-place a few
  seconds; do not compress a multi-event skill into ~1 s. If a skill fails
  `no_teleport` AND `joint_velocity_envelope` together, it is almost always too
  SHORT — increase duration_s (never a fixed value; scale it to the motion).
- RISE BEFORE YOU TRAVEL OVER A RAISED SURFACE — when the base must get ONTO a
  step/ledge/slab, do not translate the torso forward OVER the obstacle's footprint
  until the base has RISEN above that surface's top (plus folded-leg clearance).
  Author the base to go UP first, THEN forward onto the surface — the same law the
  swing limbs obey. A base that moves forward over a slab while still low drags the
  pelvis/thighs THROUGH the slab (fails surface_penetration, unrecoverably — no
  joint solve can pull a limb out of a base authored inside the solid).
- Keep commanded speeds/rotations within the physical-summary ceilings.

          "name": "<string>",
          "phase_range": [<start>, <end>],
          "starts_at": "<the EVENT at this window's start boundary, e.g.
                         'FL touchdown', 'flight apex', 'box grasped', 'start'>",
          "intent": "<what physically happens in this phase>"
        }
      ]
    },

    // WHO bears weight, WHERE, and HOW — the spine of the plan. One entry per
    // sub-phase, with EXACTLY its phase_range. "surface" = what the contact rests
    // on (floor | tread | ledge_top | none). mode: "planted" (fixed point) |
    // "pivot" (fixed point, may spin).
    "contact_schedule": [
      {
        "phase_range": [<start>, <end>],
        "contacts": [
          { "ee": "<ee_name>", "surface": "<floor|tread|ledge_top|...>",
            "surface_height_m": <number: 0.0 for the floor, else the exact height
                                 from the scene geometry>,
            "offset_from_base_m": [<dx>, <dy>],  // where this contact sits HORIZONTALLY
                                 // relative to the base at this phase (metres, +x =
                                 // the robot's forward, +y = its left). REQUIRED: a
                                 // contact can be out of reach SIDEWAYS even when its
                                 // height is fine, and no base height fixes that.
            "mode": "planted|pivot",
            "where": "<qualitative world location, e.g. 'under hip', 'on step 2',
                       'on ledge top ahead'>" }
        ],
        "notes": "<why these contacts; '[]' contacts = a flight/aerial window>"
      }
    ],

    // For every FREE (non-contact) limb THIS phase, its control channel + intent.
    // channel: "world_reach" | "body_swing" | "joint_posture" | "none".
    "free_limb_mode": [
      {
        "phase_range": [<start>, <end>],
        "limbs": {
          "<ee_name>": {
            "channel": "world_reach|body_swing|joint_posture|none",
            // REQUIRED when channel is "world_reach": the point this limb must
            // actually ARRIVE at. A grip is reach-constrained exactly like a planted
            // contact, and is checked the same way.
            "target_height_m": <number: world z of that point>,
            "offset_from_base_m": [<dx>, <dy>],  // horizontally, relative to the base
            "intent": "<what it does — 'reach onto the box', 'swing forward to
                        step', 'high front kick (joint angles)', 'arm counter-swing'>"
          }
        }
      }
    ],

    // Absolute posture of the torso over the cycle (height + orientation + travel).
    "base_motion": {
      "per_phase": [
        {
          "phase_range": [<start>, <end>],
          "base_height_m": <number: the base origin height in metres for this phase.
                            MUST satisfy the reach table in §1b for every contact
                            this phase declares — this is checked mechanically>,
          "torso_height": "<e.g. '~0.9 m upright', 'drop to ~0.4 m squat', 'rise onto ledge'>",
          "torso_orientation": "<upright | pitch forward | inverted | lean into turn | ...>",
          "torso_pitch_deg": <number: torso pitch in DEGREES. 0 = upright, 90 = torso

      ]
    },

    // Objects to animate (ONLY if the chosen environment has objects; else []).
    "object_plan": [
      {
        "object": "<object name>",

    ],

    "handoff_notes_for_implementation": [
      "<assumptions the coder must respect>",

------------------------------------------------------------
6. CRITICAL RULES
------------------------------------------------------------

- Every phase: name the contacts (possibly []) AND the channel of every free limb,
  decided by COUPLING (see §1): endpoint fixed by the world / a held-or-touched
  object → world (or body) IK; shape is the point, endpoint free → joint_posture.
- Contacts first, then base posture, then free-limb motion.
- ONE boundary set: phase_definition.sub_phases is the timeline; contact_schedule,
  free_limb_mode, and base_motion.per_phase use EXACTLY those phase_ranges, tiling
  [0,1] with no gaps and no overlaps. Every boundary sits at a named physical event;
  every width is the time its motion takes.
- A one-shot skill (cyclic=false) has DISTINCT start and end poses — do not make it
  wrap. A cyclic skill returns to its phase-0 pose at phase 1.
- Choose contact surfaces consistent with the chosen environment.
- Keep amplitudes and velocities within the physical-summary ceilings.
- Declare the fidelity_invariants (§3) that pin what makes this the intended
  motion — as many as the skill needs, no padding — the loop uses them to steer
  toward the skill, not just toward legality.

------------------------------------------------------------
7. MENTAL MODEL
------------------------------------------------------------

1. List the motion's EVENTS in order: every contact make/break, flight
   start/end, grasp/release, channel switch, apex, base reversal. These are
   the phase boundaries — one sub-phase per interval between events, as many
   as the motion has events, no more. What does the robot look like at each
   boundary?
2. Size each sub-phase by TIME, not by count: phase is normalized time, so a
   window's width is the fraction of the cycle its motion actually takes — a
   footstrike is short, a glide is long. Boundaries land where the events
   land, never at equal fractions for their own sake.
3. At each phase, which contacts bear weight and where do they plant?
4. What does the torso do (height, orientation, travel)?
5. For each free limb, which channel realizes its motion — world reach, body
   swing, or joint posture?
6. Which invariants must hold for realism, safety, and testability?

Express execution logic, coordination, and intent — never raw math.

GROUNDING & NATURALNESS — the simulator is purely KINEMATIC (no gravity). The FRAMEWORK
enforces the physics, so spend your effort on STYLE and where the contacts go:

- DURATION (set self.freq FIRST): the phase advances as (freq*t)%1, so the WHOLE skill plays
  in 1.0/self.freq SECONDS. Set self.freq = 1.0/duration_s in __init__, where duration_s is the
  plan's "duration_s" (the seconds the motion should take). DO NOT leave freq at the 1.0 default
  for anything with several events — 1.0 means the entire skill in ONE second, which forces
  every joint to move ~5-10x too fast between frames and FAILS no_teleport + joint_velocity.
  A multi-step walk/climb wants ~1 s per stance step; a pick-carry-place a few seconds; a whip
  or kick can be quick. Bigger/slower motion → larger duration_s (smaller freq). This one number
  sets every joint and base speed, so choose it from the plan, never by default.

- CONTROL CHANNELS — each end-effector, each phase, is driven by exactly ONE of three
  channels. CHOOSE per limb per phase from what the skill needs; nothing fixes a channel
  for any particular limb — decide it, don't assume it:
  * WORLD-frame IK (compute_ee_position_world_frame) — for a position the WORLD fixes:
    a contact resting on a surface, a foot planted at an absolute spot, OR a limb that must
    COINCIDE with an external thing — a hand gripping / holding / pushing an object sits AT
    that object's grip face. Author the absolute world point; IK solves the joints. A floor
    contact's target z puts the CONTACT POINT (sole/palm) ON the surface: z = surface_height
    + this EE's ground_offset (NOT z = 0, which sinks the sole a ground_offset below the
    floor and makes the reach solve mis-read the contact height). On flat ground that is
    z = ground_offset; on a tread/ledge it is tread_top + ground_offset. USE THESE EXACT
    per-contact ground_offset VALUES (do not guess or invent one — a wrong offset floats or
    sinks the contact):
{contact_offsets} If the limb holds
    an object, place the object AT the hand's point, so the limb and the object coincide BY
    CONSTRUCTION (do not drive the limb one way and then guess the object's location
    separately — they will not meet).
  * BODY-frame IK (compute_ee_position_body_frame) — for a position most naturally stated
    RELATIVE to the torso (e.g. a swing foot's arc under the moving hip). IK solves the joints.
  * JOINT angles (compute_joint_overrides) — for a limb whose CONFIGURATION is the point and
    whose endpoint is NOT fixed by anything external (an expressive gesture, a wide/deep
    stance, a kick, an inverted posture). Return None for that end-effector (IK skips it) and
    set its joint angles. Driving such a limb through a fingertip IK target dissolves its
    shape into an arbitrary reach. (The morphology card lists this robot's joint axes + signs.)
  The discriminator is COUPLING: if the endpoint is fixed by the world or by a thing the limb
  touches/holds, use IK to that point (world- or body-frame as the target is most directly
  stated); if the shape is the point and nothing external fixes the endpoint, set joint angles.
  ONE CHANNEL PER LIMB PER PHASE IS ENFORCED: if a limb has a world/body IK target this phase
  (a stance foot OR a SWINGING foot), the framework IGNORES a compute_joint_overrides value on
  that limb's joints (it would overwrite the IK solve and drive the limb open-loop THROUGH the
  floor/an object). To STYLE an IK-driven swing (a pointed toe, a bent knee), the override is a
  null-space BIAS only — do NOT try to pose an IK limb with overrides. Drive a limb by joints
  ONLY when it returns None from both IK methods. Also: use the physical-summary's reach values
  VERBATIM as your reach budgets — never invent leg/arm reach numbers (an inflated reach lets
  you author an unreachable target the IK then saturates on, dropping the limb into the surface).

- BASE: either the framework re-seats the base onto the declared contacts (contact grounding —
  then do NOT also author base height, and do not fight base motion to stop sliding), or YOU
  own the absolute base pose in world frame in update_base_motion (grounding off — then place
  the base so the declared contacts rest exactly on their surface). The environment declares
  which grounding is in effect; author to it.

- SUPPORT SET: by default this robot's ground contacts are {support_set} (free EEs: {free_ees}).
  If this skill bears weight on a DIFFERENT set, set self.support_ees=[...] in __init__,
  and/or return the per-phase load-bearing names from contacts(phase).
  Anything not in the support set is free (never grounded or pinned).
- ANY BODY CAN BE A CONTACT: besides the end-effectors, these body parts may be named in
  contacts(phase)/support_ees as weight-bearing contacts: {contact_bodies}. Use them when the
  skill rests on them — a hands-and-knees crawl bears weight on the HANDS and the KNEES (feet
  lifted), so contacts(phase) returns the hands + knees. These extra bodies are part of the
  UNION IK ROSTER: PLACE one exactly like a foot — return its world contact point from
  compute_ee_position_world_frame("<body>", phase) (z = surface + its ground_offset) and IK bends
  the limb to put it there. (You may instead shape it with joint angles, but returning the world
  point is simpler and more reliable — prefer it.)
  EVERY DECLARED CONTACT MUST ACTUALLY REACH ITS SURFACE — declaring it is not enough; the tests
  FAIL a declared contact left floating in the air (contact_grounded), even if OTHER contacts
  rest. A contact only reaches the floor if the BASE is low/near enough that the limb can span
  the gap: author the pelvis LOW enough and the torso PITCHED forward. LOW GROUND POSES GO TO THE
  BOTTOM OF THE HEIGHT RANGE — a full hands-and-knees crawl sits the pelvis near the FLOOR of this
  robot's height range ({base_height_range}), NOT mid-range: from a mid-range pelvis the arms cannot reach the floor and the
  HANDS float (even while the knees rest). Torso pitches well forward so BOTH the hands AND the
  knees rest on the floor. If a declared contact can't reach its surface from your base pose,
  LOWER/pitch/step the base until it can (do not reach the limb out to it — that floats it, see
  REACHABILITY below), or don't declare it. No body may PENETRATE a surface.
- REACHABILITY — the contact/grip point is the DATUM; move the BASE to reach it, never the
  point. A declared contact rests at a FIXED surface point (a foot on the floor, a hand on a
  ledge); a grip sits AT the object. If that point is beyond the limb's reach from the current
  base, the fix is to author the BASE so the limb root comes within arm's length of it (lower
  the pelvis / pitch the torso / step closer in update_base_motion) — NOT to move the point.
    * NEVER wrap a DECLARED CONTACT's or a GRIP's target in `self.reach_limited_target(...)`.
      That helper PROJECTS an out-of-reach point back up the limb toward the shoulder/hip — so a
      floor contact you wrapped lifts OFF the floor (the whole robot then reads as hovering and
      FAILS support_or_ballistic), and a grip you wrapped pulls OFF the object (it FAILS
      object_grip / the box levitates). Author the true surface/object point and bring the base to it.
    * reach_limited_target is ONLY for a FREE limb whose exact endpoint does not matter — a
      styling reach into empty space where you just want the arm near-straight and definitely
      reachable. If the readout reports "target missed by N cm" on a contact/grip, the BASE is
      too far from the point — lower/approach the base, don't retime and don't clamp the target.
- CONTACT HANDOFF (feet<->hands, or any change of the support set): a limb becomes a contact the
  INSTANT it arrives on the surface; the departing contact releases only AFTER it has clearly
  LEFT — OVERLAP the two sets so SOME contact is resting at every instant (never declare a contact
  that is already in the air). An unavoidable unsupported instant (a ballistic kick-up, with no
  gravity) must be kept BRIEF — shorter than the support law's flight tolerance — so it reads as a
  handoff, not a hover.
- CONTINUITY ACROSS SEAMS (no pose snaps): the pose is a piecewise function of phase, so a naive
  segment boundary TELEPORTS the joints — a limb that switches control channel (joint-override <->
  IK) or whose target jumps between two segments moves in a SINGLE frame, which the `no_teleport`
  unit test fails and which reads as an "abrupt pose change" even when the motion is otherwise
  right. Two general rules:
    * EASE and MATCH at every boundary: a segment's end value AND its rate must agree with the next
      segment's start (reuse the same smoothstep ramp; never step a value — or flip a limb's channel
      — on instantly). A transition is a physical EVENT that occupies a nonzero phase window.
    * A SWING that is about to become a CONTACT must ARRIVE at its exact foothold (position AND a
      near-final joint posture) BEFORE it is declared a contact, so the landing needs no snap. The
      framework crossfades a limb RELEASING a contact, or moving in free space, for you; it does NOT
      drag a limb that is a declared contact THIS phase (that would slide the contact), so a swing
      that lands far from its foothold still snaps — that one is yours to author.

- SCENE INTERACTION — DERIVE THE BASE FROM THE CONTACTS, NOT THE OTHER WAY ROUND.
  Every failure of these skills has the same shape: a contact is DECLARED on an object
  surface while the base is authored where a STANDING robot would be, so the contact hovers
  and `contact_grounded` fails 100% of its frames. Work in this order, per phase:
    1. Write down each declared contact's world z = (its surface height from the scene
       geometry) + (that contact's ground_offset).
    2. SOLVE the base pose that makes those reachable — do not pick a base height first.
    3. Only then author the limb targets.
  * A CONTACT THAT IS THE BASE ITSELF (the pelvis, the torso — the body the root joint is
    attached to) does NOT get "reached" by a limb: its height IS the base height. If you
    declare the pelvis resting on a seat at height H, then the authored base z for that phase
    MUST be H + pelvis_ground_offset (a seat at 0.44 m => pelvis ~0.44 + offset, NOT the ~0.9 m
    standing height). Authoring a standing base while declaring a pelvis-on-seat contact is the
    single most common way a sit fails — the pelvis floats by exactly the difference.
  * A LIMB contact (hand/foot) on a surface at height H is reachable only if that limb's ROOT
    (shoulder/hip) ends up within its reach of the contact point. Use the REACH values from the
    physical summary verbatim; if the contact is out of reach from the base you planned, MOVE
    THE BASE (lower/step nearer/pitch), never the contact.
  * NEVER declare a contact you do not also author a target for in that phase. A declared
    contact with no authored target is a guaranteed `contact_grounded` failure.
  * When the contact set CHANGES (feet -> hands, floor -> seat), the base must travel between
    the two reachable regions across a nonzero phase window, releasing the old contact only
    once the new one is planted.
- MUST-NOT (each fails a unit test):
  * NO body part may go BELOW the floor or THROUGH a surface — end-effectors AND knees, shins,
    elbows, torso. Only the intended contacts rest ON a surface, never through it. Author every
    contact at the EXACT surface height from the scene geometry. Route each swing limb so its
    whole path stays above/around obstacles: RISE the limb clear BEFORE advancing it over a step,
    and never interpolate a foot straight from the floor to a raised surface (that chord passes
    THROUGH the face). Keep the base at a coherent height per phase — do NOT dive the pelvis to
    the floor then leap it up.
  * A planted contact MUST NOT SLIDE horizontally — UNLESS it is a WHEEL, which rolls. To
    reposition a non-wheel contact, LIFT it off, move it, and set it back down.
  * Do NOT let the robot FLOAT: some contact stays on a surface at all times, except during a
    real ballistic jump/flip where the base rises then falls under gravity (accel ≈ -9.8) and
    lands. Sustained hovering fails.
